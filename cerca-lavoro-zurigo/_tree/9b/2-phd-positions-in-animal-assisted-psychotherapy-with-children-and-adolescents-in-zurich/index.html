Department of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Psychotherapy, University Hospital of Psychiatry Zurich

Start of employment January 1st, 2027, temporary

We are recruiting two PhD candidates to join the Department of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Psychotherapy (KJPP) of the University Hospital of Psychiatry Zurich (PUK). The positions are part of the interdisciplinary project “Meeting at eye level: Innovation in animal-assisted child and adolescent psychiatry and psychotherapy”, a collaboration between the KJPP Zurich and the Messerli Research Institute in Vienna, Austria. You will join an interdisciplinary and practice-oriented research team combining expertise in child and adolescent psychiatry and psychotherapy, animal ethics, cognitive biology and animal welfare science across two universities and a psychiatric clinic. The KJPP Zurich offers animal-assisted therapies at several sites, ranging from direct interactions with dogs to caring for and observing farm animals. These activities will be further expanded and supplemented by innovative therapy approaches in a new therapy house for particularly vulnerable patients (mainly with autism spectrum disorders or refugee experience). Both PhD candidates will be supervised by a postdoctoral researcher in psychotherapy and senior researchers from the participating institutions. They will receive clinical supervision and collaborate closely with the animal ethics and cognitive biology research teams in Vienna through regular online meetings and research visits. The positions offer a stimulating interdisciplinary research environment, opportunities for scientific exchange and professional development, and close collaboration between research and clinical practice. We offer two funded PhD positions (80%, 3 years) at the PUK Zurich with a yearly salary of CHF 65'000.-. Start date is January 1st, 2027. The application deadline for both positions is December 20th, 2026. University Hospital of Psychiatry Zurich Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Psychotherapy Neumünsterallee 9 8008 Zurich Hosting project The advertised position is hosted by the project „Meeting at eye-level: Innovation in animal-assisted psychotherapy for children and adolescents”. This project is a cooperation between the Messerli Research Institute (MRI) in Vienna and the University Hospital of Psychiatry Zurich (PUK) that aims to study and further develop animal-assisted interventions in child and youth psychotherapy from an interdisciplinary perspective. The project, funded by the Messerli Foundation, critically explores the potential of animals as co-therapists, taking into account the complex interests of both animals and clients. We propose a cautious approach where animals are only part of psychotherapeutic interventions if they are motivated and capable of taking on an active role in building a “therapeutic” relationship. This interdisciplinary project integrates animal behaviour and cognition, animal welfare science, animal ethics and psychology and applies philosophical-conceptual, empirical and naturalistic-therapeutic methodologies. Its research is structured as three work packages: First, we review the literature on animal-assisted interventions in psychotherapy. Second, we develop and implement therapeutic interventions that open up relevant topics with the aid of animals, without, however, necessarily relying on the use of actual animals. Instead, animals will appear in various interactive multimedia formats tailored to (1) the needs of especially vulnerable children and adolescents and (2) the overarching aim of caring and just human-animal relations. Third, we will study and evaluate these new formats as well as traditional approaches to animal-assisted therapies that involve animals more directly. We hope to advance “therapeutic” human-animal relations at eye-level that are mutually beneficial and conducive to a culture of mutual care.

Your responsibilities

  • Conduct empirical research on animal-assisted and animal-ethics-themed interventions in psychotherapy with children and adolescents, with a particular focus on social interactions and therapeutic processes, while considering animal welfare and animal ethics in interdisciplinary collaboration
  • Investigate the contribution of animals or animal-ethics-themed interventions to therapeutic processes and contribute to the assessment of their impact on participating animals and the human–animal relationship in collaboration with the research teams in Vienna
  • Collect and analyse quantitative and qualitative research data, including behavioural observations, video-based analyses, and the exploration and application of automated and AI-assisted analysis methods for behaviour patterns and social interaction dynamics
  • Publish research findings in peer-reviewed academic journals and present results at academic conferences
  • Complete a PhD thesis within the framework of the interdisciplinary research project
  • Contribute to the supervision and training of students involved in the research project
  • Actively participate in interdisciplinary and interuniversity collaboration, project meetings, scientific events and reporting activities

PhD position 1 (80%): Patient - animal interaction and therapeutic change. PhD project 1 investigates the human perspective in therapies with direct animal contact, initially with dogs. The candidate will develop observational criteria and a patient questionnaire and, in years 2-3, evaluate the therapies from a diagnostic and therapeutic perspective using video-based behaviour observation and questionnaires, with a focus on children and adolescents with autism spectrum disorder and cognitive impairments. The goal is to identify which elements of the human–animal relationship are therapeutically effective (e.g., who initiates contact, when and how; motivation of patient and therapist). A parallel PhD project in Vienna, focusing on cognitive biology, will investigate the animal perspective, including animal behaviour and welfare. The two PhD students are expected to work closely together and support each other. The common goal is to find out how themes such as respect, care and agency can be made fruitful in therapy by help of innovative, animal-ethics-themed elements, and to what extent patients and the human-animal relation benefit from them. PhD position 2 (80%): Animal - ethics therapy elements for vulnerable children and adolescents (WP2). PhD project 2 focuses on the implementation and pilot evaluation of innovative therapy elements, developed by the interdisciplinary research team, that address animals through media, observation, and play rather than direct hands-on contact (e.g., by help of a curated library of picture books and graphic novels, audio offers, a creative station, a tactile wall, a climbing structure inspired by animal nests, or interventions in the garden). The focus is on children and adolescents with refugee experience. The candidate's main tasks are to implement the therapy elements together with therapists and social workers on-site in Zurich, and to collaborate closely with the PhD student in Vienna in developing evaluation methods and conducting a pilot study assessing the benefits of these therapy elements. While the candidate employed in Zurich will be responsible for the empirical and psychological side of this research with a focus on benefits for patients, a PhD student in Vienna will be responsible for the conceptual and ethical side and focus on benefits for the human-animal relationship. The two PhD students are expected to work closely together and support each other. The common goal is to find out how themes such as respect, care and agency can be made fruitful in therapy by help of innovative, animal-ethics-themed elements, and to what extent patients and the human-animal relation benefit from them.
